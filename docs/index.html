DPhil

We find that discovery is a loosely-defined term in recommender systems literature. It is operationalised in many different ways (serendipity, ability to reach into the long tail, catalogue coverage etc), and the ability of systems to afford discovery is subject to persistent critique from fields such as media theory. We aim to approach this problem by advocating for a unified, user-centric undersanding of discovery. From this we aim to advise system design by establishing design criteria and evaluation frameworks. This allows for the creation of a discovery-oriented music recommendation system that abides by these criteia. Below are the research objectives (ROs) for this DPhil.

R01

Defining discovery in the context of music recommender systems.

As a starting point, we address the amorphous concept that is discovery; this concept can take on many meaning depending on context. We constrain our scope to music recommendation, but we aim for insights to be generalisable to other domains.

R02

Establishing clear criteria for future system design.

We understand the importance of distilling the insights of RO1 intoactionable guidelines for future design; this is the purpose of RO2.

R03

Developing a discovery-oriented recommendation system that adheres to the established design criteria.

We action all the insights obtained over the course of the DPhil.
